# Case Study Methodology — Full Reference

## 1. Headline

Options: metric-led ("How [Customer] Cut Portfolio Downtime 30% With [Product]"), outcome-led ("From Blind Spots to Full Visibility: [Customer]'s Path to Proactive Asset Management"), or problem-led ("Why [Customer] Replaced Manual SCADA Rollups — and What Changed"). Must name a specific outcome, never a feature name, and avoid vague superlatives. Produce 2-3 for the user to choose from.

## 2. Executive summary

4-6 sentences: who the customer is (industry, size, what they operate), the specific challenge in their language, the solution implemented, and the headline result. Write it to work as a standalone pull quote, since it often gets lifted into a sales deck out of context.

## 3. The challenge (before state)

3-6 bullets covering operational inefficiencies, business risk (compliance, revenue, competitive exposure), scale limitations (why the problem was getting worse, not static), and the human cost (the workarounds, the pressure on the team). Specific beats generic: "four technicians manually cross-checking SCADA exports every Monday" beats "time-consuming manual processes." Include the cost of inaction — what got worse every week the problem persisted.

## 4. Why they chose us

What alternatives were evaluated (name them if possible, or use a neutral placeholder), why they fell short on this buyer's specific priorities, and 3-5 decision drivers — for example, integration with existing SCADA/historian systems without a heavy engineering lift, faster time-to-value than a competitor's multi-month implementation, or a pricing model that scaled with portfolio growth rather than seat count.

## 5. The solution

Which capabilities were used and in what context, how it integrated with the existing stack, the deployment model, and adoption breadth. Feature-to-outcome mapping for anything mentioned: "[feature] enabled [customer] to [action], which meant [business result]." Avoid a spec-sheet feature list — every capability mentioned needs a connected outcome.

## 6. Implementation story

Rollout approach (phased vs. full, pilot site vs. whole portfolio), timeline to first value and to full deployment, adoption strategy, and friction points with how they were resolved. Change management: how the team moved from the old workflow to the new one. Acknowledging friction is what makes the rest of the story credible.

## 7. Results and business impact

Bullet format, each as "[metric]: [specific outcome] — [context or timeframe]." Categories to cover: time savings, revenue impact, cost reduction, productivity gains, risk reduction (e.g., compliance incidents avoided, fewer missed-fault events), and adoption signal. If a metric isn't available, use directional language with a qualifier and the customer's own words rather than a fabricated number.

## 8. Customer perspective

1-3 quotes that sound like a real operator: specific ("we used to spend three days reconciling site-level reports before a CNE filing; now it's automatic"), not generic ("the platform was transformational"). Match vocabulary to the persona quoted. If no real quotes exist, produce draft quotes clearly labeled "for customer approval."

## 9. Key takeaways

3-5 lessons that generalize beyond this one customer: why the solution worked in this context, what the customer did right during rollout, what a similar buyer should consider, and what surprised them — often the most compelling point.

## 10. Call to action

Match to deal stage: a peer-match CTA ("running a similar multi-site monitoring challenge? see how [product] approaches it") for awareness, a proof CTA for evaluation, or a conversation/demo CTA for late-stage.

## Multi-format repurposing

| Section | Sales deck | Email | Website |
|---|---|---|---|
| Headline | Slide title | Subject line | Page hero |
| Executive summary | Opening slide | Email intro | Meta description |
| The challenge | Problem slide | Pain-point hook | Problem section |
| Results | Proof slide | Metric highlight | Stats callout |
| Key takeaways | Closing slide | CTA setup | Insight section |

## Tone calibration

| Tone | Style |
|---|---|
| Enterprise (default) | Measured, data-driven, risk-aware |
| Story-driven | Narrative arc, emotional texture, protagonist focus |
| Technical | Architecture detail, integration specifics, performance metrics |
| Bold | Confident, direct, metric-forward |
